## 2. Architecture

`EngineCoolingSystem` is one object per `Vehicle`, therefore one per car, which is
what keeps two cars' temperatures independent. It holds no engine reference beyond
the specs it was configured with and touches nothing outside itself, which is why
the harness can drive it without a physics world.

Data is in the registry, not in code: `EngineHeatSpec` per engine (derived from the
numbers an engine already declares, overridable field by field through
`EngineSpec.heat`) and `RadiatorSpec` per radiator variant. `radiatorFit()` is the
single compatibility rule, used by the simulation AND by the bonnet prompt, so the
two can never disagree about whether a core suits an engine.

`CarState.engineTempC` is authority. The live value is mirrored in the Vehicle and
flushed on the same throttled cadence as fuel, water and oil.

## 3. Heating and cooling formula

    dT/dt   = ( heatIn - heatOut ) / thermalMass
    heatIn  = idle + load * throttle + rpm * revs                      [kW]
    heatOut = ( core * fit * airflow * water * thermostat + shell ) * (T - air)

- `airflow = 0.34 + 0.66 * min(speed / 22 m/s, 1)`. Linear from a standstill on
  purpose: a steeper curve made the first metre per second of a rolling start dump
  the temperature, which reads as a bug from the driver's seat.
- `water = min(fill / 0.7, 1)`, and exactly 0 when the core is dry — no water, no
  circuit. Only the shell loss remains, so a dry engine runs away in tens of
  seconds rather than being destroyed on the spot.
- `thermostat` is a smoothstep over `operating - 10 .. operating + 8`. It is
  load-bearing, not detail: without it the balance has one equilibrium per load and
  a correctly cooled car cruising gently settled at 52 C and never warmed up at
  all. With it the ENGINE picks the temperature at light load and the RADIATOR
  picks it under load.
- Integration is SEMI-IMPLICIT: the loss term is evaluated at the end of the step,
  which is unconditionally stable. Forward Euler on the same balance oscillates and
  then diverges once `dt * k / mass` exceeds 2, which one hitching frame reaches.
  Measured: 240 Hz, 5 Hz and 0.5 Hz agree within 2 C, and a one-hour step stays
  finite and monotone.
- Water loss: 0.55 L/h of seep while running, plus 2.2 L/h per kelvin above the
  warning temperature. An engine allowed to run hot loses water, which makes it run
  hotter. A parked car loses none.

## 4. Engines and radiators

    radiator          class      water    kW/K
    small radiator    small       5.5 L   1.10
    2102 radiator     small       6.5 L   1.10
    radiator          standard    9.0 L   1.65
    copper radiator   large      13.0 L   2.45

Requirement is full-load heat / 62 K, which maps the catalogue onto those classes:
1.2 and 1.6 fours -> small; 2.4 four and 2.8 six -> standard; 5.0 V8 and 6.6 truck
diesel -> large. Petrol engines run 90 C (warning 110, critical 125, maximum 140);
diesels 95 C (115 / 135 / 155).

Fitment is never refused. An undersized core loses a further tenth of its rating
(it also flows badly for the pump) and warns; an oversized core is capped at a
fifth above what the engine asks for, so it buys water capacity and thermal margin
but not a magically cold engine.

## 5. Game-loop integration

`Vehicle.fixedUpdate` builds the context from applied throttle, `rpm / redline`,
forward speed and `ambientAirC(timeOfDay)`, then applies the result as a torque
scale and a rev ceiling through `Drivetrain.setThermalLimits` — the one place torque
is produced, so nothing else in the vehicle needs to know temperature exists.
`settle(dt)` runs the same model with no combustion, so parked cars cool down.
`rebuild()` rebinds engine and radiator, so a bonnet swap takes effect on the next
tick and water that does not fit the smaller core is spilled.

There is no weather system in this game and this feature did not justify one:
ambient air is a cosine about a daily mean, 14 C at dawn to 46 C at 15:00, lagged
past noon like real ground-heated air.

## 6. HUD

A fourth dial in `.hud-gauge-row` (tach, speed, fuel, temperature, clock), built
with the existing `buildDial` helper and cached in the constructor like every other
dial — no element is created per frame. Per frame it writes the arc/needle through
`updateDial`, a whole-degree `92 C` label through the existing diff guard, and one
of `.hud-temperature.is-cold|is-normal|is-warm|is-hot|is-critical` (blue, green,
yellow, orange, flashing red). `temperature: null` (no engine fitted) hides the
gauge and produces no warnings. The overheat text joins the existing diff-guarded
lamp string beside `NO WATER` and `OIL LOW`.

## 7. Adding an engine

Add the variant to `ENGINE_VARIANTS` as before. It gets a working cooling profile
automatically from its fuel, peak power and cylinder count. Disagree with any part
of it by adding `heat: { warningC: 118, thermalMassKjPerK: 45 }` — anything omitted
stays derived. Nothing else needs touching: fitment, the gauge scale and the zones
all read the profile.

## 8. Adding a radiator

Add a variant with `kind: 'radiator'` and
`radiator: { klass, capacity, coolingKwPerK }`. `preferredRadiatorClass`, the
factory fitment table, the mesh switch in `partmesh.ts` and `radiatorFit` all key
off `klass`, so a new core in an existing class needs no other change. A new CLASS
means one entry in `RADIATOR_CLASSES`, one in `CLASS_CAPABILITY`, one in
`FACTORY_RADIATOR` and one mesh branch.

## 9. Scenarios verified

`tools/cooling.ts` (28 checks, all pass) covers all ten scenarios from this brief:
small engine + small core + full water stabilises at 86 C; big engine + small core
reaches 194 C under load; big engine + large core holds 96 C; dry core passes
critical within 60 s; a 30% core runs 12 K hotter than a full one; standing still
runs 18 K hotter than moving; a stopped engine decays to air temperature; a marginal
radiator that copes at 14 C air cooks at 46 C; 240 / 5 / 0.5 Hz agree within 2 C;
an incompatible core reports a readable reason. Plus warm-up time (93 s), the zone
order hot -> critical -> seizure, power cut before seizure, recovery on lifting off,
boil-off, NaN/Infinity input, no engine, no radiator, water spill on downsizing and
two systems not sharing state.

`tools/cooling-drive.ts` (17 checks, all pass) proves the wiring with real
Vehicles: a factory V8 pickup holds 103 C flat out in 46 C air; the same car on a
small core reaches 119 C, loses 35% of its torque, drops its rev ceiling to 0.82,
boils water and stalls; pulling the radiator takes its water with it and running
with no radiator heats rather than crashes; fitting a full large core hands the car
its water and capability; a parked car cools 35 K in ten minutes and persists it;
two cars in one world keep independent temperatures.

Also re-run green: `tools/car-dirt.ts`, `tools/autosave.ts`,
`tools/runtime-lifecycle.ts`. `tsc --noEmit` clean. The gauge was checked visually
in all five zones and the four radiator meshes were checked in `tools/coollab/`.

## 10. Known limitations and deliberate behaviour changes

- Dry water no longer destroys an engine instantly. It overheats, warns, loses
  power, stalls, and only seizes after six seconds past its maximum temperature.
- Water capacity is the RADIATOR's, not the engine's. Old saves keep their litres,
  clamped to the fitted core.
- One roadside POI car in four carries a deliberately undersized radiator. It
  drives away fine and will not hold temperature on a long pull.
- A cold engine loses up to 10% torque. An earlier 28% was measured and reverted:
  every drive starts cold, and it moved the whole catalogue's acceleration figures.
  `tools/handling-bench.ts` (measurement only, no assertions) will read a few per
  cent slower over the first 90 s of a run.
- No leaks, no fan, no head-gasket damage, no coolant other than water. The seams
  for all four exist (`RadiatorFit`, the loss term, `WATER_BOIL_LPH_PER_K`,
  `FluidKind`) and none are implemented.
- Water cans hold 5 L, so refilling a 13 L core takes three of them.
- Ambient air is a function of the clock alone: no shade, no altitude, no wind.
